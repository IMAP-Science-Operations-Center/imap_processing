.. _lo-l2:

Level 2 - Calibrated Science and Sky Maps
=========================================

.. include:: /algorithm-code-documentation/_ai_generated_notice.inc

**Module:** ``imap_processing/lo/l2/lo_l2.py`` (~1900 lines), on top of the
shared ``imap_processing/ena_maps/`` machinery.

L2 turns counts and exposure into **ENA intensities on a sky map**, with the
instrumental corrections applied. This is where the most science-sensitive code
lives, and where the code and the algorithm document diverge most.

.. important::

   **Appendix A supersedes the main body.** The algorithm document says
   explicitly that all formulas and values in "The IMAP-Lo Mapping Algorithms"
   (Appendix A, v6, Schwadron et al., pages 209-226) take precedence over
   section 12. Where numbers differ between section 12 and Appendix A, use
   Appendix A. Equation numbers below refer to Appendix A. **[DOC]**

Products the document defines
-----------------------------

**[DOC]** Section 12 defines six L2 products:

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 30 15 55

   * - Product
     - Cadence
     - Description
   * - ENA fluxes, S/C frame
     - 5-7 days
     - A strip of sky, 60 spin bins x 7 ESA levels. No corrections.
   * - Corrected ENA fluxes, Sun rest frame
     - per pointing
     - Sputter + bootstrap + Compton-Getting corrected, HiRes only.
   * - ENA spectra in selected directions
     - -
     - 1 AU energy spectra along named look directions.
   * - S/C frame ENA flux maps
     - 1 year
     - Full sky, 6 x 6 degrees, light and heavy atoms.
   * - 1 AU ENA flux maps
     - 1 year
     - Full sky, 6 x 6 degrees, HAE, Compton-Getting corrected, light atoms.
   * - ISN fluxes / rates
     - -
     - 6-degree spin bins per energy step, by elongation angle.

**[CODE]** The repository implements **one** of these: a rectangular sky map
whose exact character is selected by the map descriptor. There is no separate
"strip of sky" product, no spectra product, and no ISN product. See
:ref:`lo-implementation-status`.

How ``lo_l2`` actually works
----------------------------

**[CODE]** ``lo_l2(sci_dependencies, anc_dependencies, descriptor)``:

1. Parse the descriptor into a ``MapDescriptor``. Raise ``NotImplementedError``
   for any species other than ``h`` and for HEALPix tiling.
2. If the map is Compton-Getting corrected, build the
   ``PowerLawFluxCorrector`` from the ``esa-eta-fit-factors`` ancillary **up
   front**, so a map missing it fails before any work is done.
3. Assemble complete pointings: ``_complete_pointings`` keeps only repointings
   that have all of ``goodtimes``, ``bgrates`` and ``histrates``.
4. Determine the ESA mode from the last pointing's histrates; the whole map is
   binned in that mode's energy response.
5. Load calibration: ``_esa_calibration(species, esa_mode)`` reads the
   geometric factor ancillary and yields per-level energy, geometric factor,
   and low/high geometric factor bounds.
6. If ISN masking is requested, resolve mask tuning for the map's pivot angle
   up front; fail early if there is none.
7. ``_accumulate_pointing`` for each repointing, projecting spin-angle bins to
   sky pixels.
8. ``_calculate_rates_and_intensities`` derives everything and applies the
   corrections in order.
9. ``_build_map_dataset`` and ``finalize_dataset``.

Accumulators
^^^^^^^^^^^^

.. code-block:: python

   ACCUMULATED_VARIABLES = ("ena_count", "exposure_factor", "bg_rate_exposure")
   # plus, conditionally:
   #   "sputter_source_count"  when sputter corrected
   #   "cos_alpha_exposure"    when Compton-Getting corrected

Everything else is derived from these after all pointings are in.

Projection and spin phase
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

* ``_dps_spin_angles`` gives the spin angles in the despun frame.
* ``_spin_phase_mask`` selects ram / anti-ram / full according to the
  descriptor.
* ``_ram_projection(spin_angles, pivot_angle)`` handles the ram geometry,
  using ``LoConstants.PIVOT_RAM_OFFSET = 4.0`` degrees as an empirical offset
  added to the measured pivot angle.

The intensity calculation
-------------------------

**[CODE]** ``_calculate_rates_and_intensities``. Written in the code's own
terms:

.. math::

   R = \max\!\left(\frac{C_{\text{corrected}}}{T},\; 0\right),
   \qquad
   \delta R = \frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(C_{\text{corrected}})}}{T}

.. math::

   I = \frac{R}{G \, E},
   \qquad
   \delta I = \frac{\delta R}{G \, E}

where :math:`T` is the accumulated ``exposure_factor``, :math:`G` the
geometric factor (cm^2 sr keV/keV) and :math:`E` the ESA level center energy
(keV). Every quotient is taken only where ``exposure > 0``; unexposed pixels
get ``FILLVAL_FLOAT = -1.0e31``.

**Systematic error** comes from the geometric factor error bounds rather than a
single sigma. Because :math:`I \propto 1/G`, the *lower* G bound gives the
*upper* intensity:

.. math::

   I_{\text{upper}} = \frac{R}{G_{\text{low}} E}, \qquad
   I_{\text{lower}} = \frac{R}{G_{\text{high}} E}

.. math::

   \sigma^{+} = I_{\text{upper}} - I, \qquad
   \sigma^{-} = I - I_{\text{lower}}

with a symmetric error taken as the geometric mean of the two. Levels whose
lower G bound is non-positive get zero systematic error and a warning.

**[DOC]** The document's equivalents are Appendix A equations 2-4:

.. math::

   \langle J \rangle_{\text{pxl}} = \frac{\sum_i C_i/\epsilon_i}{G E \sum_i \tau_i},
   \quad
   \langle (\delta J)^2 \rangle_{\text{pxl}}
     = \frac{\sum_i C_i/\epsilon_i^2}{\left[G E \sum_i \tau_i\right]^2},
   \quad
   \langle \sigma J \rangle_{\text{pxl}} = \frac{\sigma_G}{G}\langle J\rangle_{\text{pxl}}

Note the document divides counts by the MCP efficiency :math:`\epsilon_i` per
contribution, and takes a single fractional systematic error. The code instead
carries asymmetric bounds from the geometric factor ancillary and applies
efficiency separately. **[CODE deviates]**

Correction 1: Sputter
---------------------

Physical origin: an oxygen atom hitting the conversion surface sputters
hydrogen, which is then counted as a hydrogen ENA at a lower energy than the
parent oxygen.

**[DOC]** Appendix A eqs. 8-12, applied at ESA levels 5 and 6 only for the
90-degree map:

.. math::

   \langle J_k^c \rangle = \langle J_k \rangle - \langle J_k'^O \rangle f_k^O,
   \qquad
   f_5^O = 0.15 \pm 0.05, \quad f_6^O = 0.01 \pm 0.008

**[CODE]** ``_sputter_correction`` / ``_sputter_correct_counts``. The
correction is applied **to counts, not intensities**, and uses a full
(target level, source level) matrix from
``ancillary_data/imap_lo_sputter-correction-factors_v002.csv``:

.. code-block:: text

   source  target  target_esa  source_esa  factor
   o       h       1           4           0.236
   o       h       2           4           0.372
   o       h       3           4           0.898
   o       h       4           4           0.891
   o       h       5           4           0.037
   o       h       5           6           0.32
   o       h       6           6           0.32
   o       h       7           6           0.22

.. math::

   C^{c}_{t} = C_{t} - \sum_{s} M_{ts} \, C^{O}_{s},
   \qquad
   \mathrm{Var}(C^{c}_{t}) = C_{t} + \sum_{s} M_{ts}^{2} \, C^{O}_{s}

implemented as two ``np.einsum("ts,esp->etp", ...)`` contractions. The variance
only grows, which is correct for Poisson counting in both species. Negative
corrected rates are clipped to zero.

This is a **substantial generalization** of the document: seven target levels
instead of two, and coefficients that do not match :math:`f_5^O` or
:math:`f_6^O`. Treat the CSV as authoritative and the document as historical.

Correction 2: Bootstrap
-----------------------

Physical origin: hydrogen also sputters to lower energies, so a level's counts
include leakage from higher levels.

**[DOC]** Appendix A eqs. 16-34. Recursively, from the top level down:

.. math::

   J_b^7 = J^{c'}_7 - h_{78} J_b^8, \qquad
   J_b^k = J^{c'}_k - \sum_{m=k+1}^{8} h_{km} J_b^m

with the virtual level 8 extrapolated as a power law from the top two levels:

.. math::

   J_b^8 = J^{c'}_7 \left(\frac{E_8}{E_7}\right)^{\gamma},
   \qquad
   \gamma = \frac{\ln(J^{c'}_6 / J^{c'}_7)}{\ln(E_6/E_7)},
   \qquad E_8 = 2.1\, E_7

Uncertainties propagate as
:math:`(\delta J_b^k)^2 = (\delta J^{c'}_k)^2 + \sum_m h_{km}^2 (\delta J_b^m)^2`,
and systematic errors scale as :math:`\sigma J_b^k = (J_b^k / J^{c'}_k)\, \sigma J^{c'}_k`.

**[CODE]** The coefficient matrix in
``ancillary_data/imap_lo_bootstrap-correction-factors_v001.csv`` **matches
Appendix A Table 3 exactly** (0.03, 0.01, 0.05, 0.02, 0.01, 0.09, ..., 0.52,
0.061, 0.75). ``ESA_8_ENERGY_RATIO = 2.1`` also matches.

The code adds machinery the document does not have:

* ``BOOTSTRAP_SCALE = 0.5`` - the nominal coefficients are **halved** before
  being applied. ``BOOTSTRAP_SCALE_INTENSITY_HIGH = 0.25`` and
  ``BOOTSTRAP_SCALE_INTENSITY_LOW = 1.0`` bracket that choice and become the
  **systematic error** on the corrected intensity.
* ``_local_median_spectral_index`` - a pixel with no measurable spectral index
  borrows the median from a
  ``BOOTSTRAP_SPECTRAL_INDEX_FILTER_SIZE = 3`` pixel neighborhood.
* ``BOOTSTRAP_DEFAULT_SPECTRAL_INDEX = 1.6`` - used when the map offers no
  index at all.
* ``_extrapolate_top_intensity`` - the virtual level, with the derivation
  written out in its docstring:

  .. math::

     I(E) = A E^{-\gamma}, \quad
     \gamma = -\frac{\ln(I_{\text{top}}/I_{\text{second}})}
                    {\ln(E_{\text{top}}/E_{\text{second}})}, \quad
     I_{\text{virtual}} = I_{\text{top}}
       \left(\frac{E_{\text{virtual}}}{E_{\text{top}}}\right)^{-\gamma}

  The normalization :math:`A` cancels, which is why a pixel can borrow a
  neighbor's index and still anchor on its own top level.

Correction 3: Solar frame transform and Compton-Getting
-------------------------------------------------------

This is the most mathematically involved step. It has two parts that the
document sometimes conflates.

Part A: kinematic frame transform
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

**[DOC]** Appendix A eqs. 36-47. The spacecraft moves at :math:`u \approx 30`
km/s, so the ENA velocity in the heliospheric inertial frame is

.. math::

   \vec{v}^{\,h} = \vec{v}^{\,s} + \vec{u}

With :math:`\alpha` the angle between the boresight and the ram direction, and
:math:`E^u = \tfrac{1}{2} m u^2 \approx 4.7` eV for hydrogen,

.. math::

   \frac{E^{h}}{E^{s}}
     = 1 - 2\frac{u}{v^s}\cos\alpha + \left(\frac{u}{v^s}\right)^{2}

Phase space density is invariant, so differential flux transforms with the
energy ratio (eqs. 41, 43-47):

.. math::

   J^{h} = J^{s} \frac{E^{h}}{E^{s}}, \qquad
   \delta J^{h} = \delta J^{s} \frac{E^{h}}{E^{s}}, \qquad
   \sigma J^{h} = \sigma J^{s} \frac{E^{h}}{E^{s}}

and the same factor applies to background flux. Exposure-weighted mean inertial
energy per pixel (eq. 42):

.. math::

   \langle E^h \rangle_{\text{pxl}} = \frac{\sum_i E^h_i \tau_i}{\sum_i \tau_i}

**[CODE]** ``LoConstants.CG_ENA_ENERGY_AT_SPACECRAFT_SPEED_EV = 4.661`` eV.
``_spacecraft_frame_energy(cos_alpha, energy)`` solves the inverse relation
(Appendix A eqs. 49-50):

.. math::

   y_k^2 = x_k^2 - 2 x_k \cos\alpha + 1, \qquad
   x_k = \cos\alpha + \sqrt{y_k^2 - \sin^2\alpha}, \qquad
   E_k^{s} = x_k^{2} E^{u}

which is why ``cos_alpha_exposure`` is accumulated during the pointing loop.

Part B: ESA transmission and the source spectrum
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

**[DOC]** The ESA passes a range of energies, so what is observed is the true
source spectrum convolved with the ESA response. A steep spectrum biases the
response toward low energies. The bias factor is a 5th-order polynomial in the
local power-law index :math:`\gamma`:

.. math::

   \eta_{\mathrm{esa},k}(\gamma) = M_0 + M_1\gamma + M_2\gamma^2
     + M_3\gamma^3 + M_4\gamma^4 + M_5\gamma^5

with coefficients per ESA level in Appendix A Table 4 (document page 222).
Recovering the source spectrum requires iteration, because :math:`\gamma` is
computed from the fluxes and the fluxes depend on :math:`\eta(\gamma)`.

The **predictor-corrector** (Appendix A eqs. A1-A10):

.. math::

   \gamma^{(0)}_k = \frac{\ln(J_{k+1}/J_k)}{\ln(E_{k+1}/E_k)},
   \qquad \gamma^{(0)}_7 = \gamma^{(0)}_6

.. math::

   J^{\text{src},(0)}_k = J_k / \eta_{\mathrm{esa},k}(\gamma^{(0)}_k)

then alternate a predictor step (recompute :math:`\gamma` from the source
fluxes, average with the previous) and a corrector step, until

.. math::

   \chi^{n} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N_{\text{esa}}}
     \sum_k \left(\frac{J^{\text{src},n}_k}{J^{\text{src},n-1}_k}\right)^2 - 1}
     \; < \; 0.5\%

or 20 iterations. Typically converges in <= 16 even for rugged spectra.

Finally (eqs. 55-58):

.. math::

   J^{g}_k = J^{\text{src}}_k \left(\frac{E^{s}_k}{E_k}\right)^{\gamma_k + 1}

.. math::

   (\delta J^{g}_k)^2 = (J^{g}_k)^2\left[
     \left(\frac{\delta J^{s}_k}{J^{s}_k}\right)^2 (1+\gamma_k^2)
     + \left(\frac{\delta J^{s}_{k+1}}{J^{s}_{k+1}}\right)^2 \gamma_k^2\right]

For :math:`k=7` use :math:`\gamma_6`.

**[CODE]** ``LoConstants.CG_MAX_ITERATIONS = 20`` and
``CG_CONVERGENCE_TOLERANCE = 0.005`` match. The implementation is split:
``_power_law_slopes``, ``_source_intensity``,
``_compton_getting_correct_intensity`` in ``lo_l2.py``, with the
:math:`\eta` polynomial evaluation in
``imap_processing.ena_maps.utils.corrections.PowerLawFluxCorrector``, fed by
the ``esa-eta-fit-factors`` ancillary. **The polynomial coefficients are not
hardcoded**; they come from the ancillary, and the map fails to build without
it.

**[DOC]** The correction applies to HiRes mode only, and levels below 4 need
careful examination before release because they may be background dominated.

Correction 4: ISN mask
----------------------

**[CODE only - not in the algorithm document.]** ``_isn_mask``.

Interstellar neutral hydrogen is bright enough to swamp the heliospheric ENA
signal, and arrives as a band along the ecliptic. The mask blanks out, per ESA
level:

1. **The band** - pixels that are both bright
   (``intensity >= intensity_threshold_fraction * peak_of_level``) and near the
   ecliptic (``abs(elevation) <= angular_width_deg``).
2. **The outliers** - pixels above the level's own
   ``outlier_percentile``, which catches ISN pixels off the plane.

Crucially, the mask is computed from the **uncorrected** intensity, so a map is
masked identically regardless of which corrections its descriptor asks for.

Tuning is per (pivot angle, ESA level) in
``ancillary_data/imap_lo_isn-mask-parameters_v001.csv``, which only covers
pivot angles 75, 90 and 105. A map at any other pivot angle raises
``ValueError``. Levels 6 and 7 have ``intensity_threshold_fraction = 1.0``,
i.e. effectively only the single brightest pixel plus outliers.

``ISN_MASKED_VARIABLES`` lists the eight variables blanked out: the counts and
everything derived from them.

Order of operations
-------------------

**[CODE]**

.. code-block:: text

   accumulate counts / exposure / bg_rate_exposure per pointing
     -> sputter correction        (on counts)
     -> rate = counts / exposure  (clipped at 0)
     -> intensity = rate / (G * E)
     -> systematic error from G bounds
     -> bootstrap correction      (on intensity)
     -> Compton-Getting           (on intensity)
     -> ISN mask                  (from uncorrected intensity)
     -> fill unexposed pixels with FILLVAL_FLOAT

Background handling
-------------------

**[DOC + CODE]** Background is **never subtracted**. It is carried alongside as
``bg_rate``, ``bg_rate_stat_uncert``, ``bg_intensity`` and
``bg_intensity_stat_uncert`` so users can subtract case by case. The document
gives the reason: background characterization is hard, especially at the low
energies IMAP-Lo newly reaches, and premature subtraction biases statistics.

Appendix A eqs. 5-7:

.. math::

   \langle J_B \rangle_{\text{pxl}} = \frac{\sum_i J_B^i \tau_i}{\sum_i \tau_i},
   \quad
   \langle (\delta J_B)^2 \rangle_{\text{pxl}}
     = \frac{\sum_i (\delta J_B^i)^2 \tau_i^2}{\left[\sum_i \tau_i\right]^2},
   \quad
   \langle \sigma J_B \rangle_{\text{pxl}} = \frac{\sigma_G}{G}\langle J_B \rangle_{\text{pxl}}

ISN products
------------

**[DOC only.]** ISN products are **rates**, not fluxes, because a given signal
mixes species. No geometric factor is applied; only the MCP efficiency.

Appendix A eqs. 59-64:

.. math::

   \langle R \rangle_{\text{pxl}} = \frac{\sum_i C_i/\epsilon_i}{\sum_i \tau_i},
   \qquad
   \langle (\delta R)^2 \rangle_{\text{pxl}}
     = \frac{\sum_i C_i/\epsilon_i^2}{\left[\sum_i \tau_i\right]^2}

.. math::

   \langle \sigma R \rangle_{\text{pxl}}
     = \frac{\sum_i \sigma_{\epsilon_i} \tau_i}{\sum_i \epsilon_i \tau_i}
       \langle R \rangle_{\text{pxl}}

and for backgrounds, the same exposure-weighted forms. Three products are
specified: ISN Rates (6-degree spin bins, per pivot angle), ISN 1AU Maps
(6 x 6 degrees per map index, an elongation-angle range), and ISN Pointing
Event Lists (per-event records within ISN goodtimes, used to derive TOF
branching ratios and hence species abundances).

**None of this is implemented.**

Level 3
-------

**[DOC only.]** L3 divides an L2 map by a survival probability map
(Appendix A eq. 35):

.. math::

   J^{sS}_k = \frac{J^{sA}_k}{P^{s}_{\mathrm{sp},k}}

with the same division applied to :math:`\delta J`, :math:`\sigma J` and the
background terms, so the map and its background stay consistent. At least two
independent survival probability models are required, and the maps themselves
go in the product metadata. Inputs likely come from GLOWS. Not initially
public. **Not implemented.**

Map naming reference
--------------------

Appendix A designates maps as
:math:`J_k[\text{frame},\text{corr},\text{SP},\text{res},\text{mode}]`:

* frame: ``s`` spacecraft, ``h`` heliospheric inertial, ``g`` Compton-Getting
* corr: ``n`` none, ``c`` sputter only, ``b`` sputter + bootstrap
* SP: ``S`` survival-probability corrected, ``A`` at 1 AU
* res: ``6`` or ``4`` degrees
* mode: ``r`` HiRes, ``t`` HiThru

The repository uses the IMAP-wide ``MapDescriptor`` grammar instead (see
:ref:`lo-data-products`). The two do not map onto each other one-to-one.
